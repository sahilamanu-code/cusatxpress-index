CUSAT enforces an unambiguous **zero-tolerance policy** on ragging. Ragging in any form — physical, verbal, psychological, electronic, or otherwise — is strictly prohibited on campus, in hostels, and in any location connected to university activities. The university takes all complaints seriously, acts swiftly on every report, and imposes strict disciplinary measures, including suspension or expulsion, on those found guilty. If you are a new student, you are required to submit an **anti-ragging undertaking** at the time of admission before you can complete your enrolment.

<Warning>
  Ragging is a criminal offence under the directions of the Hon'ble Supreme Court of India and UGC regulations. Perpetrators face not only university disciplinary action but also legal prosecution.
</Warning>

## Anti-Ragging Cell

The **CUSAT Anti-Ragging Cell** is the designated body that receives, investigates, and acts on all ragging-related complaints. You can access the cell's information, submit complaints, and track their status at:

**[cusat.ac.in/student/antiragging](https://cusat.ac.in/student/antiragging)**

The Cell is composed of faculty members, senior administrators, and student representatives who are trained to handle complaints with sensitivity and discretion.

## Mandatory Anti-Ragging Undertaking

Every new student — undergraduate, postgraduate, and doctoral — must complete the anti-ragging undertaking as part of admission formalities. The undertaking is available on the university's online admissions portal and must also be submitted in physical form at your department on the day of reporting.

<Note>
  Parents or guardians of freshers are also required to sign the anti-ragging undertaking. Enrolment cannot be finalised until both the student's and parent/guardian's signed copies are received by the department.
</Note>

## How to Report Ragging

If you experience or witness ragging, you can report it through any of the following channels — choose whichever you are most comfortable with:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Contact the Anti-Ragging Cell Online">
    Visit [cusat.ac.in/student/antiragging](https://cusat.ac.in/student/antiragging) and use the complaint submission form. You can file a complaint anonymously if you prefer, though providing your contact details helps investigators follow up with you.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Call the National Anti-Ragging Helpline">
    Dial **1800-180-5522** — a toll-free, 24-hour helpline operated by the Ministry of Education, Government of India. Your call is treated confidentially, and action is coordinated with the university.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Approach the Warden or Hostel Authorities">
    If the incident occurs in or near a hostel, report it immediately to the Warden on duty. Wardens are mandated to escalate all ragging complaints to the Anti-Ragging Cell without delay.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Contact University Administration Directly">
    You may also approach the university administration — the Registrar's office or the Dean of Student Affairs — if you feel the situation requires immediate senior-level attention.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Consequences for Ragging

The university treats no complaint as trivial. Depending on the severity of the incident, disciplinary action may include:

- Formal written warning placed on academic record
- Suspension from classes or hostel for a specified period
- Cancellation of hostel accommodation
- Rustication (temporary expulsion) from the university
- Permanent expulsion and cancellation of admission
- Reporting the matter to law enforcement authorities for criminal proceedings

<Info>
  Senior students are encouraged to actively support freshers during the orientation period and to immediately report any ragging activity they observe. Bystander inaction can also be treated as complicity under university regulations.
</Info>
