On the other hand, the rapid development of information technologies has led to the global computerization of society. The result of this phenomenon in the education field is a stable environment formation of developed data processing, information systems modular construction (combination of different architectural types within a single complex). As a result, knowledge building process for skills formation of professional competence is gradually changing the form of its realization. The well- known learning goals of higher school revealed in such elements as knowledge, understanding and forms of their acquisition are changing. It is no coincidence that the problem for most universities is the fact that education is still more focused on knowledge and skills, rather than on personality development, consciousness formation and self-awareness of a specialist. Unfortunately, such changes do not always have a positive educational trajectory. It should be recognized that educational institutions are no longer the major educational source. Those basic learning goals, the achievement of which is the task of higher school, do not receive appropriate positive reflection in society. High level of knowledge with no skills of its application is the main factor of professional deformation of a graduating student of higher educational institution. This problem is clearly visible in the higher legal education sphere. In recent times, there has been a clear trend toward the transition from qualification to the competence-based model of the specialist. The emphasis in professional training is shifting to socio-personal and general professional competencies. In early 2019, the Ministry of Justice of Ukraine together with the Ministry of Education and Science of Ukraine proposed a draft Concept of the Legal Education Reform for public discussion. Among the main problems were a mismatch between the content of legal education and training quality in legal schools with the modern requirements of the labour market and the challenges facing the modern democratic society, global trends in development and tasks of employees’ professional activities in various fields. The abovementioned requires a change in the educational paradigm, one of the components of which, in our opinion, should be the transition to educational goals of a different qualitative type in the cognitive sphere, including the formation of applied professional legal skills, critical thinking development and so on. Unfortunately, there is not enough scientific research in this area of narrow thematic nature. No significant attention is paid to the pedagogical problems of lawyers’ model formation with their readiness for social interaction. It is important to emphasize that among legal scholars there is no interest in the development and formation of legal pedagogy as a direction in pedagogy of higher education, the subject of which is the didactic peculiarities of the professional competence formation of lawyers. Part of the attention is paid to the professional competence formation among students of higher educational institutions in the legal field by scholars of pedagogical science, in particular, the theory and methods of training and education (according to the fields and educational level). The problem to be solved is the mismatch between the content of legal education and training quality in legal schools with the modern requirements of the labour market and the challenges facing the modern democratic society, global trends in development and tasks of employees’ professional activities in various fields, this is due to the insufficient level of theoretical knowledge and practical skills among a significant part of graduating students of law schools, as well as a lack of attention to the formation of a holistic world view among applicants for legal education with an understanding of the social mission, values and importance of legal profession to approve the rule of law in the modern democratic society. -- 7 of 41 -- SLS News Letter Optimus Omnium Vol. 1 Issue 1 2021 3 The abovementioned requires the updating of educational processes modernization issue in the direction of competency-oriented training formation and professional lawyer education in the light of his/her dialogue and discussion, and language-intensive professional activities. Guest Article Prisoners’ Right to Health Care Prof. (Dr.) N. S. Soman Former Director & Dean School of Legal Studies Cochin University of Science and Technology, Kerala A convict sentenced by competent court is a persona non grata and dumped inside the prison to be forgotten by the law and legal system. Law is of no help to him since he is “civilly dead”. His status as state slave and a person met with “civil death” disentitles him of any assistance from the law courts in case of any injury done towards him. The courts take a “hands off” stand, which is a corollary stand based on the doctrine of “civil death”. New developments in penology as well as the concept of rights of individuals created inroads into this traditional stand of courts. It is insisted that the public being responsible for deprivation of the liberty of the convict, indisputably it is under an obligation to take care of the prisoner, since he could not do it himself. Such an obligation would enable the prisoners to lead a dignified life. The prisoner, the state slave, now stands emancipated and no longer neglected to receive only the rights granted at the whims of prison authorities. Courts abandoned "hands-off" doctrine and no longer reject prisoner’s complaints about legal wrongs as necessary conditions of imprisonment. New constitutional interpretations never allowed the writs to stop at the prison gate. -- 8 of 41 -- SLS News Letter Optimus Omnium Vol. 1 Issue 1 2021 4 Prisons being made out of the bricks made of law the constitutional writs could easily pierce it and review the legality/illegality which prevails inside. The prisoner no longer continues to be a persona non grata, and the fact of incarceration will not deprive him of the rights which are consistent with the fact of imprisonment. All things which ensures a decent life inside the prison is sine qua non of the prison atmosphere to be in tune with constitutional norms. Among many facets of rights of prisoners emanating from the constitutional order right to health care forms one prominent component. Courts through the interpretation of constitutional rights starts spinning out human rights of prisoners to claim medical assistance in cases of need. Deprivation of this constitutional mandate of right to health care gives rise to cause of action to subject to judicial review and give appropriate direction in cases of violation of rights. Though the scope and extent of review as well as the level of medical care for which the prisoner is entitled is inconclusive and controversial, no one will deny the existence such a right in present position of law. International as well as regional human rights mechanisms accept right to health care for prisoners as an aspect of human rights enjoyable by the Jurisprudence from various national jurisdictions also found to be in tune with the international trend. In India though there is no direct precedent specifically addressing rights of prisoners to get medical care, the trend of the constitutional jurisprudence logically supports such a claim. In this context it is imperative for the legal system to ensure transparent mechanism to ensure that health care is provided to all prisoners in need of the same. The requirements in prison manuals shall be scrupulously implemented to ensure appointment of medical personnel as part of the prison administration so that a prisoner could get immediate medical attention in case of need. It should be legally mandated that the prison authorities shall record medical condition of the prisoner at admission and shall ensure continued treatment in cases of prisoners having medical problem. Periodical medical checkup of prisoners shall be ensured and proper records shall be kept in the prison. It is unfortunate to see that many of the prisoners has to fight for getting medical care that too after proving their medical condition. Instead of insisting the prisoner/petitioner to prove his health condition necessitating medical assistance the courts shall insist the prison authorities to produce medical records with an opportunity for the prisoner to controvert it with other independent evidence of -- 9 of 41 -- SLS News Letter Optimus Omnium Vol. 1 Issue 1 2021 5 by praying for review by an independent medical board. This will avoid the possibility of death of a prisoner due to deterioration of health and the consequent helplessness of the courts except to feel shocked. Prison deaths due to delay in getting medical assistance due to inhuman attitude of administration shook not only the conscience of the court but the entire community. This evidences the lightness by which the basic people are handled by the administration. It is time to see that our legal system evolves ways and means to facilitate the enjoyment of constitutionally guaranteed fundamental rights especially that of the prisoners’ right to healthcare. Keeping in mind the fact that it is the prison rules and jail manuals that are followed by the authorities while dealing with the prisoners, and their healthcare, it is also imperative that the rights of prisoners that are engraved from the existing fundamental rights are incorporated into these rules. S. Seema Associate Professor School of Legal Studies Cochin university of Science and Technology Not many areas are unaffected by the pandemic Covid-19. Surmounting the rich and the poor, powerful and powerless, and crossing all barriers of religion, and caste, this microorganism has left the whole world in the shade of fear, anxieties, uncertainties, and what not! Like any other sector, the education is also very badly affected. Thanks to the digital technology, this difficult situation is somehow managed. Education through online platform raises financial, intellectual, social, cultural, mental and physical challenges to the givers and takers alike. It is equally painful and shocking to hear the news of students committing suicide due to lack of access to online classes. Teachers also find it very difficult to cop up with the new virtual experience. But, it is a matter of fact that the human mind will adjust to anything in due course. Adjustment to the new type of imparting education by the stakeholders is not an exception. This paper examines the nuances of Legal Education (class room teaching and examination) in the online mode as a substitute to the traditional class due to the pandemic situation. Class Room Teaching Recording of video lecturers of teachers and uploading of the same on You Tube platform or similar platforms for the students to attend at their own convenience, giving lectures on digital platform for the students to listen, and even the facilities to interact with the teachers (MS-Team, cisco webex, Zoom, Google meet, Google classroom etc.) are all available. Given the fact that this happens out of a helpless situation, this arrangement should be appreciated and -- 13 of 41 -- SLS News Letter Optimus Omnium Vol. 1 Issue 1 2021 9 is better than not to have classes at all. With the growth of online imparting of education, proportionate growth can be seen in the technology to make it almost at par with the traditional style of teaching. Using of chat box, white board, giving opportunities for the students to interact are some of the techniques suggested. There are sumptuous videos giving training to teachers how to teach effectively in an online class. There are also videos giving details of how to teach Law. Presentation from the front of the class, taking attendance, track participation, hang out assignments and quizzes, proctor exams, and talking one to one with a student are suggested as measures to make virtual class rooms feel like real class rooms. This makes it clear that teaching part has become very much advanced in online mode thanks to the fast growing technology. It can be expected that in future, there will be 3D platforms for the students and teachers to really feel each other. However, the accessibility to and affordability of these technologies by the teachers as well as students are grave challenges. Examinations When it comes to the question of online examinations, the challenge is, how to test the student’s knowledge in the subject even if the student can have access to the materials. Hitherto in the traditional offline exams, it was the memory of the students that was mostly tested and such questions cannot hold well in the online mode. On line examinations are in a way a blessing in disguise for us to switch over to a better system, and to get rid of a rotten system. With the coming of on line examinations, we are compelled to find a substitute for the unscientific method of the examination system we have today. It is an outdated pattern of examination to ask some questions and to write the answers. Because in this process the real knowledge of the student cannot be tested. Neither is this system effective to find out a knowledgeable student. For example, if the question asked for an examination of the Law of Torts is, write a short note on Donoghue v Stevenson and the answer written is, “The manufacturer owes to their final consumer a duty of care and that there need not be a contractual relationship for final consumer to file for negligence” , the answer seems very good. But, it is not possible to find out whether the student has read the case or at least a comment of the case. A guide type book will help the students answer this. But, in order to really test the knowledge of the student, questions -- 14 of 41 -- SLS News Letter Optimus Omnium Vol. 1 Issue 1 2021 10 like facts of the case, reasons for the decisions, the name of the judges, etc. will have to be asked and assessed. The questions need not be questions- they can be projects, and any other measure in the application level that can enable the student to use the knowledge in an application level. When it comes to online examination, the question answer type becomes completely irrelevant, much so a question like Write short note on Donoghue v. Stevenson because invigilation for an online exam is an unnecessary component and there are limitations for the same. It is always better to allow the students to go through the materials and apply it. What is needed is to find out whether the student has achieved the “learning outcome” of the course. So if one of the learning outcomes of the course is to inculcate in the students an aptitude to write articles, it can be tested only by giving the student a project to write article and to get it published. The Blooms Taxonomy levels of Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate and Create are now applied to learning outcomes in the Outcome-Based education. In the opinion of Mr. Kuncheria P. Isaac, the Vice Chancellor , Hindustan Institute of Technology & Science, today’s examination system tests only the first three (remember, understand and apply) in which the capacity to remember and reproduce alone are tested. All the levels need to be tested. So according to him, for the first three levels the assignment and assessment shall be test, quiz, review etc. and for the higher levels, it shall be study, reports, experiment, models, projects, peer and self-evaluation. This is not an exception in the case of legal education as well. In order to assess whether a student has understood the expansion of the concept of “State “under Article 12 of the Constitution of India, the best way is to ask the student to write an article in the area including the latest trends. A question like “ Examine the contribution of the Supreme Court of India in expanding the horizons of the concept of State under Article 12” and an answer to the same is not enough to find out whether the student has actually understood the application of the precedents in each case. With the awarding of a mark and declaration that the student has passed the exam, the matter ends there. What is needed is to create a generation of Law students who read the cases in original, and thus understand the whole picture. This is not to be confined to the post graduate level. It is a -- 15 of 41 -- SLS News Letter Optimus Omnium Vol. 1 Issue 1 2021 11 misconception that undergraduate Law students need only study the minimum and the higher levels can be done in the post graduate level. It is better to give them the insight into the depth of the subject when they are young and vibrant itself. Given the fact that after under graduation they are qualified to be judicial officers, advocates or legal officers, (and thus the implication that post graduate degree in Law is not needed for these), it is always desirable that after completion of LL.B. itself, the student knows the jurisprudence, philosophy and case law developments in depth in all subjects they learn. This will ensure a good Bench and a Bar. It is undesirable to conduct online examinations with the traditional pattern of examination. In fact such written examinations are to be done away with and what is needed is the test of “to what extent the learning outcome’ is achieved by the students”. This is also an opportunity for all involved in education, including legal education to think of testing the students in all levels. That will give way to a better generation of students who will be interested in learning rather than considering education as an imposition, as practical application of what they learn from the books is also done. The message is, this exercise is not to be discontinued once the actual classes start. This is to be continued…and let the students enjoy learning. And thus the teachers enjoy teaching also. Let the ostensibly bad time of the world due to the pandemic turn to have been for the betterment in future! Student Note Weighing Hits and Misses of Draft Anti-Trafficking Bill, 2021 Bisna Chandran 9th Semester, BBA. LLB (Hons.) School of Legal Studies “Though a man be a slave, he is the same flesh as thyself; for no one has ever been born a slave by nature, but fortune subjected his body to servitude” Human trafficking is a bane, an open wound on the body of humanity. It's not only a crime in itself but also the foundation of several other crimes. A nation -- 16 of 41 -- SLS News Letter Optimus Omnium Vol. 1 Issue 1 2021 12 cannot be called civilized if it tolerates the buying and selling of its beings. Covid -19 pandemic has had unprecedented repercussions for human rights globally. It has heightened the trafficking in India due to the increased unemployment and the financial distress caused by it, prolonged closure of the educational institutions, and death of members of family. Cash advances are made, and economically vulnerable groups are exploited in various corners of the country. Many workers are tricked into low paying jobs. The pandemic period saw a surge in debt-based coercion. Online grooming also increased during the pandemic. Children now spend more time online for virtual learning without any supervision of their parents, which makes them more vulnerable to attacks. The government is also juggling and shifting its priorities in this pandemic period. It focused more on the health sector to reinstate the collapsed economy. Regarding the measures to combat the menace of human trafficking, efforts were made to establish Anti-human trafficking units across the country. Women help desks in the police stations is also a brainchild of these efforts. Increased patrolling at transportation hubs and railway stations, courts accepting video testimony of the victims, identifying and increasing the compensation of the victims of bonded labour are all some positive steps. However, India still has a low convictions rate when it comes to human trafficking cases. We continue to prosecute human trafficking cases under the Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection) Acts (2000 and 2015) The Indian Penal Code, The Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act etc. Coping with an offence of such gravity calls for nicety from the country. Passionate chants of thousands of survivors, leaders, civil societies and spirited people demanding comprehensive anti-trafficking legislation have resulted in the recent Draft Trafficking in Persons (Prevention, Care and Rehabilitation) Bill, 2021 proposed by the government of India. The first bill introduced in the Lok Sabha in 2018 failed to reach the upper house, and this is the 2nd iteration of the Bill. The Bill aims to deal with all aspects of trafficking, including social and economic causes of the crime, punishment to traffickers. It also aims to provide protection and ensure the rehabilitation of the survivors. This draft has a glimmer of hope and some concerns surrounding it. The draft of the Bill states that the National Investigation Agency (NIA) shall act as the investigating and coordinating agency. -- 17 of 41 -- SLS News Letter Optimus Omnium Vol. 1 Issue 1 2021 13 It would be the body responsible for the prevention and tackling of human trafficking and other offences which would come within the purview of this Act. NIA will also deal with the investigation, prosecution and coordination in cases of trafficking in persons. However, shifting the responsibility to National Investigation Agency has been widely criticized, stating that it would overburden the already strained department. Moreover, these shifts will also airbrush the local enforcement agencies out of the picture. Many NGOs have come forward with contentions that the draft bill is silent on India’s online commercial sexual activity. They demanded that online commercial sexual exploitation must be included within the definition of "offences with cross border implications”. Objections were also raised as the proposed bill states that the victim's consent could be immaterial and is likely to criminalize sex work and the choice of sex work as a profession because the Bill has given an extensive definition to victims. Other concerns that were raised with respect to this bill include the draconian penal provisions like the death penalty for some offences and a heavy-handed approach that does not precisely address the root cause of trafficking. Bill doesn't mention anything about community-based rehabilitation and funds that need to be allocated to rehabilitate survivors, which is also causing worries. All these while human trafficking has existed, subverting the laws that have imposed restrictions on the whole practice making the act of human trafficking an inescapable reality of the present-day world.
