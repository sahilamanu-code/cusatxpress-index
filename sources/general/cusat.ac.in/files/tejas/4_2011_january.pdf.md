Editorial... It is with great pride and pleasure that I am writing this piece about the special convo- cation organised at the Cochin University of Science & Techno logy. I would like to share the ecstasy of the CUSAT Dr. Ramachandran Thekkedath fraternity on the occasion of the special convo- cation by publishing this special issue. On the 4th Special Convocation in the history of our University, we are indeed privileged to confer honorary docto- rates (honoris causa) on three eminent personali- ties who have made an indelible mark in their respective professions. And it requires special mention that the contribution of each of them has helped to enrich the scientific, social and cultural fabric of our great nation. Needless to say, the esteem of our University is immeas- urably enhanced by honouring these disting- uished personalities and at the same time we are meticulously following and embellishing our tradition of honouring great sons of our magnificent country who have made valuable contributions in the frontier areas of science, technolo- gy, humanities, social science and performing arts. The practice of awarding honorary degre- es dates back to the middle ages. The earliest honorary degree on record was awarded by the University of Oxford in the late 1470’s. Many universities around the world continue to award honorary doctorate degrees to the high achievers and disting- uished personalities in recognition of their achieve- ments. It is our firm belief that through this associati- on with persons of repute our university has a lot to gain in terms of reputation & goodwill. At this moment of pride, I wish to recall the three earlier occasions when we had such august personalities in Cusat Vice Chancellor Dr. Ramachandran Thekkedath is receiving His Excellency Sri R.S. Gavai, the Governor of Kerala and Chancellor of the University. Cusat Vice Chancellor Dr. Ramachandran Thekkedath is receiving Sri. M.A. Baby, Hon’ble Minister for Education and Pro - Chancellor of the University. our midst. Prof. E C G Sudarshan who is an internationally acclaimed Physicist was the first in the history of CUSAT to be conferred with the honorary degree of Doctor of Science on 18 th February, 1992. The second special convo- cation was held on 1 st February 1997 to confer honorary degree of Doctor of Law on the then Chief Justice of India, Mr. Justice A. M. Ahmadi. On 15 th January 2006, the third special convocation was held to confer honorary degrees on Sri. G. Madhavan Nair and Sri. N. R. Narayana Moorthy. On the 4th such grand ceremony, the University has had the unique privilege of honouring three persona- lities hailing from entirely different fields. While Dr. E. Sreedharan has gained fame and reputa- tion from the field of science & technology, we have Dr. K G Balakrish- nan from the area of law & justice and Dr Kalamandalam Raman- kutty Nair from the field of performing arts. I am certain that this special edition of Tejus with its colourful images and thought-provoking words will be a good experience to the readers. I hope this volume, commemorating a milestone in the annals of the glorious history of our university, will be enjoyed by all our well- wishers. Justice K G Balakrishnan Your Excellency: Justice.K.G Balakrishnan, formerly the Chief Justice of India and currently the Chairman of the National Human Rights Commission embarked on his career in the legal field as an advocate of the Kerala Bar Council and went on to scale great heights both as an advocate and as a judge, eventually attaining the highest post in Indian Judiciary, namely the Chief Justice of India. On retirement in May 2010, he was appointed as the Chairman of National Human Rights Commission, which also is a great recognition of his dedication to the law of the land and unflinching commitment to the depressed and downtrodden in the country. During his tenure in the Supreme Court, he delivered several landmark judgments. These included an order which made the mid-day meal programme in schools a statutory requirement. This order brought relief to millions of poor children throughout the country who had to discontinue their studies due to poverty. Very recently, a Division Bench headed by him held as unconstitutional forcible narco- analysis, polygraph and brain electrical activation profile or brain-mapping tests by the investigating agencies which were raising concerns on the human rights of the people. He was awarded honorary Doctorates by several Universities including Kurukshetra University, University of Mysore, University of Kerala and University of Bangalore. He was also awarded first Sri V K Krishna Menon Award for his fight against corruption in the judicial system and for championing social causes. From childhood he had the great Pattikkamthodi Ravunni Menon as his guru and started professional training in the art form at Kerala Kalamandalam from the tender age of 13. He also spent several years teaching at his alma mater Kerala Kalamandalam and also served as its Principal. Shri Ramankutty Nair is acclaimed for his portrayal of the anti-hero ‘Kathi” roles. His ‘Ravana’, ‘Duryodhana’, ‘Keechaka’ and ‘Cheriya Narakasura’ are unparalleled for their power and perfection. Under his tutelage numerous artistes have attained excellence in the dance form and gone on to popularize kathakali all over India and abroad. I am sure, the university has selected me for this honour, in recognition of the service I had rendered to the timely completion of the Konkan Railway and the Delhi Metro Project. These two mega projects were completed on time and within the estimated costs. Konkan Railway is a 760 km. long Broad Gauge line from Mumbai to Mangalore, along the West Coast of India. It passes through one of the most difficult terrains ever encountered in the history of railway construction in this country. Konkan Railway has changed the life style and travel pattern of the people of the Konkan region. Delhi Metro project is being hailed as the proudest achievement of modern India. Delhi Metro has shown to the world that India is capable of planning and executing technically complex mega projects to international standards. These two projects are shining examples of how Government projects can be completed on time and without cost over-runs. By honouring me with the Honorary Degree of Doctor of Science, the university has honoured the engineers who had helped me to successfully Punctuality is the cardinal principle of work culture ... complete these two projects. On their behalf I accept this honour. What is that which has made Konkan Railway and the Delhi Metro to stand different from other Government projects? It is not merely the size of the projects or the technical complexities. It is the style and manner in which they were handled that made them different. These two organizations, viz. the Konkan Railway Corporation and the Delhi Metro Railway Corporation, have a very different style of handling projects. They have a unique Work Culture and Organizational Values. The cardinal pillars of the Work Culture are:- Punctuality, Integrity, Professional Competence, and Social accountability. Punctuality is nothing but courtesy and consideration for the time of others. Punctuality enabled adhering to the tight time schedule for completion of works. In regard to “Integrity”, for us it does not mean just “honesty” or lack of “corruption”. Integrity covers the whole range of our activities. For us integrity means “having good moral values” and “absolute transparency” in whatever we do. On the corruption index scale, Transparency International has put India as the 85 th most corrupted country. We should all be ashamed and alarmed at the scale of corruption taking place in this country. This is India that was Bharath. Where are all our age-old traditions and the great values and virtues handed over to us through our Scriptures and Spiritual texts? If India has to gain its past glory and prosperity, mere economic growth or GDP growth alone is not enough. “SATHYAMEVA JAYATHE” is enshrined in our Constitution. We have to now practise “SATHYAAD PARAM NAASTI” – there is nothing greater than TRUTH. It is the Institutions like the Cochin University of Science and Technology that should instil and imbibe good Values and Virtues in our student community so that when their turn comes, they can manage and rule the country in the most upright style and standards. The need for “professional competence” in handling projects does not require any emphasis. The country produces 2 million engineers a year. The best of them go abroad for higher studies and do not return. The next best, switch over to Management or IT Sector, with the result, at the ground level, there is an acute shortage of good and competent engineers. There is a need for our technical institutions to improve the quality of technical education and instil a pride for their profession. Our Prime Minister has set a target of one trillion billions of dollars (i.e. 44 lakh crore of rupees) to be spent for infrastructure creation in the 12th Plan period. Where are the engineers needed to handle competently so many projects? Now I come to the 4th pillar of our Work Culture – “Social accountability”. In a project “Time is money”. Each day the project is delayed, the cost goes up. In Delhi Metro Phase-II the cost of delay was calculated as Rs.2.5 crore per day. We are using the Tax Payers’ money for implementing projects. The project authorities should realize, they have a responsibility to provide the “best value” for the money entrusted to them. The projects have to be completed not only on time but within the budget. While doing so, they should ensure high quality and standard of work, and execute the project causing the minimum inconvenience or dislocation to the people with the least damage to ecology. Delhi Metro is a standing example to this. We have created a world-class metro network, covering 180 kms. in Delhi, in a short period of just 12 years with frontline technologies. Today Delhi is the fastest growing Metro System in the world and what is more, the most profitable Metro. I wish the Work Culture and Values of Delhi Metro spreads to all areas of Government functioning. The makers of the Indian Constitution were fully aware of and alive to the need for providing safeguards for the weaker sections of society as is evident from the Preamble to the Constitution and Part IV of the Constitution, that is, the Directive Principles of State Policy, to ensure the creation of a just society in which human rights of all individuals would be respected and protected. The NHRC, in tandem with this spirit of the Constitution, has relentlessly endeavoured to be at the vanguard of the battle to curb violations of human rights across the country and create an enabling environment for the realization of not only civil and political rights, but also social and economic justice, in its short journey of over 16 years since its inception. For the Commission, the defence of human rights has become the defence of democracy itself – a democracy that is inclusive in character and caring in respect of its most vulnerable citizens. In furtherance of its task to better protect and promote human rights, the NHRC has realized that failures in the sphere of human rights in economic, social and cultural areas are widespread across the nation and these denials have often driven the citizens to the margins of human existence. While the Commission received a mere 496 complaints regarding human rights violations in 1993, the year of its establishment, during 2009-2010, the Commission registered 82,021 fresh cases and completed action in 86,050 others. While National Human Rights Institutions like the National Human Rights Commission, India have a significant role to play in the promotion and protection of human rights in all spheres including social, economic, political and cultural, they can at best work as catalysts in assisting efforts towards protecting and promoting human rights of the most vulnerable groups. The contributions of civil society actors, notably academics and professionals, are just as crucial in this noble endeavour. To this end, I believe, education has a crucial role to play in spreading a culture of human rights by making students, academics and the public at large, more conscious of individual and community rights and fundamental freedoms and empowering them to stand in defence of these. Human rights education can spread knowledge of the instruments that exist at the international and national levels to promote and protect human rights, in addition to developing knowledge, skills and the requisite attitudes, necessary to defend these rights and freedoms. It is only through an informed public discourse on deepening and widening the ambit of human rights that a rights culture can be ushered into the country. Further, human rights education can promote values, beliefs and attitudes that will encourage respect for the rights of the self and others, and, in the long- run, play a potent role in preventing human rights abuses and more importantly, creating a just society in which the rights of all individuals and communities are protected and promoted. It is in this context that I believe, schools and universities, such as this esteemed institution, can play a vital role in making human rights and a humanistic philosophy the core of their academic and extra- curricular pursuits. I would once again like to thank you for this honour, and the opportunity to share some of my thoughts with this August gathering today. I wish the University and its students the very best for all their future endeavours. May you become protagonists of a bright and just future for all of humanity.
