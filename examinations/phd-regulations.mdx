Pursuing a PhD at CUSAT means undertaking doctoral research under a structured framework of academic regulations designed to uphold quality, rigour, and fairness across all departments. These regulations govern everything from the eligibility criteria for admission to the final thesis submission and viva voce process. If you are planning to enrol in a doctoral programme — or are already a registered scholar — understanding these regulations is essential.

<Note>
The complete **PhD Regulations document** is the authoritative source for all rules and procedures. Always consult the official document before making decisions about enrolment, coursework, or thesis submission. Access it at [cusat.ac.in/student/phd-regulations](https://cusat.ac.in/student/phd-regulations).
</Note>

## Eligibility for Admission

To be eligible for a PhD programme at CUSAT, you must hold a Master's degree in a relevant subject from a recognised university. The minimum marks requirements are:

| Category | Minimum Marks in Master's Degree |
|---|---|
| General / OBC | 55% |
| SC / ST | 50% |

Admission to doctoral programmes is typically based on the **DAT (Doctoral Admission Test)**, with rank lists published department by department. Check the relevant department's notifications for DAT schedules and results.

<Info>
DAT rank lists are published individually for each department. Keep an eye on your department's announcements and the CUSAT official website for the release of rank lists after the test.
</Info>

## Programme Duration

The minimum duration for completing a PhD at CUSAT depends on whether you are enrolled as a full-time or part-time scholar:

| Mode | Minimum Duration |
|---|---|
| Full-Time | 3 years |
| Part-Time | 4 years |

There is no guarantee of completing within the minimum period — the actual timeline depends on the progress of your research, feedback from your doctoral committee, and timely completion of all requirements.

## Compulsory Coursework

During the **first year** of your doctoral programme, you are required to complete a compulsory coursework component. This typically covers:

- **Research Methodology** — approaches to academic research, literature review, and research design
- **Subject-specific courses** — as prescribed by your department and doctoral committee
- Other foundational topics required to strengthen your research foundation

Successful completion of this coursework is a prerequisite for progressing to independent research work.

## Annual Progress Reports

You are required to submit **Annual Progress Reports (APRs)** throughout the duration of your PhD. These reports are reviewed by your supervisor and doctoral committee to assess the progress of your research and provide structured feedback. Failure to submit APRs on time can affect the continuation of your registration.

## Open Defence / Pre-Submission Seminar

Before you can submit your thesis, you must conduct a **Pre-Submission Seminar (Open Defence)** — a formal, open presentation of your completed research to the university community. This step is mandatory and must be completed with the approval of your supervisor and doctoral committee.

The open defence serves as a quality checkpoint, giving peers, faculty, and the wider academic community an opportunity to engage with your research before it is formally submitted. See the [Open Defences](/examinations/open-defences) page for more on what this involves.

## Thesis Submission

You may submit your thesis only after:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Complete All Coursework">
    Finish the compulsory first-year coursework as prescribed by your department and doctoral committee.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit Annual Progress Reports">
    Ensure all required APRs are submitted and cleared for the duration of your programme.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Conduct the Open Defence">
    Successfully complete the pre-submission seminar (open defence) and address any feedback from your committee.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Obtain Supervisor and Committee Approval">
    Receive formal approval from your research supervisor and doctoral committee confirming the thesis is ready for submission.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Submit Your Thesis to the University">
    Submit the final thesis in the prescribed format to the Examination Wing / Research and Development Cell as per the current regulations and deadline.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The Research and Development Cell (RDC)

The **Research and Development Cell (RDC)** at CUSAT oversees the entire PhD ecosystem — from admissions and registration to progress monitoring and thesis evaluation. If you have procedural questions about your doctoral programme, the RDC is your primary administrative point of contact alongside your supervisor.

## Common Questions

<Accordion title="Can I pursue a PhD at CUSAT while working full-time elsewhere?">
Yes, CUSAT offers a **Part-Time PhD** mode for working professionals, with a minimum duration of 4 years. You must still fulfil all coursework, APR, and thesis requirements. Check the official regulations for residential norms and attendance requirements applicable to part-time scholars.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What is the DAT, and how is it conducted?">
The **Doctoral Admission Test (DAT)** is the entrance test for PhD admissions at CUSAT. It is conducted department by department, and rank lists are published separately for each department. Watch official CUSAT announcements for test dates, syllabi, and results.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Where can I find the list of current PhD scholars at CUSAT?">
Visit [cusat.ac.in/PhDScholars.php](https://cusat.ac.in/PhDScholars.php) for a listing of registered PhD scholars across all departments.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What happens after my thesis is submitted?">
After submission, your thesis undergoes external evaluation by examiners appointed by the university. Once the evaluation reports are received and found satisfactory, you will be called for a **Viva Voce** examination. Successful completion of the viva voce leads to the award of the PhD degree.
</Accordion>
