One of the distinctive features of doctoral research at CUSAT is the **Open Defence** — a pre-submission seminar that every PhD scholar must conduct before submitting their thesis. These sessions are open to everyone: faculty members, fellow research scholars, postgraduate students, and any member of the university community who wishes to attend. They offer a transparent, collegial environment for scholarly exchange and academic critique.

## What Is an Open Defence?

An Open Defence is a formal seminar in which a PhD scholar presents their completed research to an open audience before the thesis is officially submitted for evaluation. It serves as a critical pre-submission checkpoint where the scholar:

- Presents the scope, methodology, findings, and conclusions of their doctoral research.
- Receives questions, observations, and feedback from the audience and an expert panel.
- Addresses any gaps or suggestions before finalising the thesis for submission.

This process ensures that CUSAT doctoral theses meet rigorous academic standards and have undergone transparent peer review within the university.

<Note>
The Open Defence is a **mandatory** step in the CUSAT PhD process. Scholars must complete this seminar and receive approval from their supervisor and doctoral committee before submitting their thesis to the university.
</Note>

## Who Can Attend?

Open Defences at CUSAT are open to all — you do not need a special invitation or prior registration to attend. Whether you are:

- A **research scholar** looking to understand how peers are structuring their work,
- A **postgraduate student** exploring research areas for your own future doctoral journey,
- A **faculty member** interested in the research happening across departments, or
- A **curious student** keen on a particular subject area,

you are welcome to attend any scheduled Open Defence at CUSAT.

<Tip>
Attending open defences in your field is a great way to understand the depth and rigour expected of doctoral research, discover potential supervisors, and learn how to present and defend academic work confidently.
</Tip>

## Finding the Schedule

CUSAT publishes the schedule of upcoming Open Defences on a dedicated page, updated regularly as new defences are announced. Each listing typically includes:

- Name of the PhD scholar
- Department and School
- Title of the research / thesis
- Date, time, and venue of the open defence

📅 **[View the Open Defences Schedule → cusat.ac.in/open_defences](https://cusat.ac.in/open_defences)**

The page also maintains an **archive of past defences**, which you can browse to get a sense of the research landscape across CUSAT departments over the years.

<Warning>
Open Defence dates are announced on a rolling basis — often just **a few weeks in advance**. If you want to attend a session, check the schedule page regularly so you do not miss an upcoming defence in your area of interest.
</Warning>

## Department-Wise Scheduling

Open Defences are organised department by department throughout the year, meaning there are sessions from different schools and disciplines at various points in the academic calendar. There is no single annual event — they happen as and when scholars are ready to defend, making the schedule dynamic and spread across the year.

## Common Questions

<Accordion title="Do I need to register in advance to attend an open defence?">
Generally, no prior registration is required — these sessions are open to all. However, if a specific department asks attendees to inform them in advance for seating or logistics, that information will be mentioned in the announcement on [cusat.ac.in/open_defences](https://cusat.ac.in/open_defences).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I ask questions during the open defence?">
Yes. The open nature of these sessions means the audience is usually invited to ask questions after the scholar's presentation. The format may vary slightly between departments, but academic engagement from attendees is encouraged.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Where are open defences held?">
The venue is listed alongside each defence on [cusat.ac.in/open_defences](https://cusat.ac.in/open_defences). Defences are typically held within the relevant department's seminar hall or a central facility on the CUSAT campus.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="I am a PhD scholar — how do I schedule my own open defence?">
Coordinate with your research supervisor and the Research and Development Cell (RDC) at CUSAT. Your supervisor will guide you through the procedure for scheduling your pre-submission seminar. Refer to the [PhD Regulations](/examinations/phd-regulations) for the complete requirements around the open defence step.
</Accordion>
